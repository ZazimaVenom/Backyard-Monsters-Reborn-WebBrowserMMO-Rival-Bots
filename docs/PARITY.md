# How 0.13.0 compares with Backyard Monsters

**0.13.0 is a playable local multiplayer remake with substantial differences from the Flash release.** On one PHP/MySQL server, players and 1,000 persistent bots build yards, earn and spend resources, hatch armies and invade. The source inventory contains 140 numbered building and yard-object definitions, including five placeholders, and 27 ordinary monster definitions. The release implements all 27 species and all 84 decorative catalog entries. The game has regional worlds and shared event rounds. Map scale, historical troop formations, progression tuning, some platform services and parts of the audiovisual presentation differ from the Flash release.

The comparison uses the supplied Refitted client 128.5690 and server 1.4.4. Those sources include edition-specific rules and inactive calendar entries. A definition may describe a holiday object, a portal, a territorial core or a decoration, so numbering alone cannot establish gameplay or animation coverage. ## Building, attacking and repairing

| System | Playable here | Differences from the recovered game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yard and workers | Place, move, construct and upgrade buildings and walls. Harvest four resources, train and research monsters, assign bunkers, reinforce eligible structures and save two surface layouts. **Repair base** immediately restores completed buildings for free. | The surface Town Hall has ten levels. Original late-Hall costs and times use the local resource and clock scales. Limits, footprint geometry and construction animation differ from Flash. Regional field yards have their own planner. |
| Rival yards and rankings | Scout a player or bot from Invasions, visit their yard, then start a time-limited raid. Load owned troops into a Flinger bucket and click a valid point on the map. Towers, traps and defenders fight automatically. Ruins persist. Ranked winners gain 25 points and losers lose 15. | Flinger weight and the five-second firing cycle follow source rules. Simulation advances in quarter-second steps. Exact Flash frame timing, unit movement and every original damage calculation differ. |
| Weapons and defenses | Ground, airborne, low-flying and burrowing units, pathfinding, bunkers, traps, splash, armor, siege devices and Catapult ammunition have distinct effects. New defenses include Railgun, Quake, Magma and Inferno/Blast towers and both earned Spurtz Cannons. The Chaos Lab and Factory research and produce all three devices through ten levels. | Effective ranges, jobs, prices and health use documented conversions. The local Lab follows one source ruleset. Magma and other attack effects resolve on the local clock. |
| Bots | The installer seeds exactly 1,000 identifiable bots at zero rank and creates no human account. Signed-in human presence activates simulation. Bots build different layouts, collect, hatch, research, fight and repair under the same paid economy. Advanced bots can use siege devices, join clans, capture land or open an underground yard. | Bots and the shared online-only scheduler are part of the requested remake. When the last human lease expires, bot production and bot-origin combat pause. A later login resumes them without awarding catch-up ranks. |

## Artwork, animation and audio

All 27 ordinary species have six painted bearings and two reflected bearings. They also have separate stride and attack artwork. The four Cage guardians have six evolutions each, and Krallen has three power appearances in the art catalog. The release adds 216 painted defeat poses and 324 painted directional stride and attack poses. A fall uses one illustrated camera angle and a reflection. Many in-between motions and combat effects are calculated by the renderer. The 224 post-level-one building files contain 208 distinct illustrations, including surface Town Hall levels 6–10. Production machinery animates independently inside the finished buildings.

The six original music loops, 29 common sound effects, and 64 creature arrival and defeat calls play locally. Illustrated events, camera moves, and new animations reproduce part of the original presentation. Exact Flash movement, every directional fall, every building animation, the historical cinematics and soundtrack, and complete translation of every dialog remain outside verified parity. The six-language guide and translated core controls are available from the game itself.
